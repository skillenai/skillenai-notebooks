Everyone negotiating a remote job worries about the same thing: that going remote means getting paid wherever they happen to live.

It doesn't. We checked 46,873 US tech postings with real salary ranges.

Where you live barely registers. Where your employer is registers enormously.

Take the identical job: remote, senior, software engineer. Same rung, same role, same job board. A company anchored in California posts it at $217K. A company anchored in Maryland posts it at $159K.

That's $57,082 for the same work. Wider than a full promotion.

And it is not the state doing the work. Once you know which company is hiring, adding the state to the model explains almost nothing extra: 0.005 against 0.262 for the employer. Roughly fifty to one. We checked that against a placebo with the same number of fake employers, and the placebo explained nothing, so it isn't a modelling artifact.

Inside a single employer, the gap between states is only about 8%.

So the thing people optimize hardest is the thing that matters least. Moving to Austin does not reset your band. Taking the offer from the Austin company does.

Two other things fell out of it:

The remote pay premium everyone quotes is fake. Remote postings advertise 6% more, but that vanishes once you compare like role with like role. Remote hiring just skews senior.

And we cannot tell you the leftover gap with a straight face. Our three posting sources disagree: one says remote pays 2.3% less, one says no difference, one says 5% more, and the intervals don't overlap. So we publish the disagreement instead of a fake point estimate.

If you have taken a remote offer: did you check where the company sets its bands before you negotiated, or after?
