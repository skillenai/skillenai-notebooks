Everyone negotiating a remote job worries about the same thing: that going remote means getting paid wherever they happen to live.

It doesn't. We checked 46,873 US tech postings with real salary ranges.

Where you live barely registers. Where your employer is registers enormously.

Take the identical job: remote, senior, software engineer. Same rung, same role. A company anchored in California posts it at $217K. A company anchored in Maryland posts it at $159K.

That's $57,082 for the same work. Wider than a full promotion.

And it isn't the state doing the work. Once you know which company is hiring, the state explains almost nothing on top, roughly fifty to one in the employer's favour. Inside a single employer, the gap between states is about 8%.

So the thing people optimize hardest is the thing that matters least. Moving to Austin does not reset your band. Taking the offer from the Austin company does.

Dylan Hughes at Sequoia looked at this from the employer's side and found something that clicks into place next to it. Across 581 companies, AI companies are far more likely to be fully remote than other software companies at every size, 25% against 15% under fifty employees, and the gap closes as they grow. His read: remote is a lever companies pull to compete for talent they'd otherwise lose to better cash and equity.

Which explains why it shows up as roughly zero in the pay data. Remote is a recruiting instrument, not a pricing instrument. Nobody is charging you for it and nobody is paying you extra for it. So stop negotiating over it, and go look at who is making the offer.

If you've taken a remote offer: did you check where the company sets its bands before you negotiated, or after?
