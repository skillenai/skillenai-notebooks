Everyone negotiating a remote job worries about the same thing: that going remote means getting paid wherever they happen to live.

It doesn't. We checked 46,873 US tech postings with real salary ranges.

Where you live barely registers. Where your employer is registers enormously.

Take the identical job: remote, senior, software engineer. Same rung, same role, same job board. A company anchored in California posts it at $217K. A company anchored in Maryland posts it at $159K.

That's $57,082 for the same work. Wider than a full promotion.

And it isn't the state doing the work. Once you know which company is hiring, adding the state explains almost nothing extra: 0.005 against 0.262 for the employer. Roughly fifty to one. We tested it against a placebo of fake employers, which explained nothing at all.

Inside a single employer, the gap between states is only about 8%.

So the thing people optimize hardest is the thing that matters least. Moving to Austin does not reset your band. Taking the offer from the Austin company does.

Dylan Hughes at Sequoia has been tracking the employer side of this in his Comp & Benefits, Plotted newsletter: companies have stopped spreading raises evenly and now funnel merit dollars into a narrow set of high-impact roles. He is looking at what companies budget. We are looking at what they advertise. Same direction, different window.

One more thing, and it cuts against us: we can't give you the leftover remote gap with a straight face. Our three sources disagree, one says remote pays less, one says no difference, one says more, and the intervals don't overlap. So we published the disagreement instead of a tidy number.

If you've taken a remote offer: did you check where the company sets its bands before you negotiated, or after?
