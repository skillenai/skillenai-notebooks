Somewhere along the way, "researcher" became an insult in AI hiring.

You have read the line. "This is not a research role." "Not just prototypes." One posting listed it under a heading that said You Might Not Be A Fit If: "your experience is limited to experimentation without production systems."

If you are a data scientist or an ML engineer, you have probably started editing yourself because of it. Leading with "shipped to production." Burying the eval work. Mentioning the PhD late, if at all.

Don't.

We read 62,659 US tech job postings with real descriptions, from 8,728 employers.

- The line is rare. It appears in 2.7% of AI Engineer ads and 0.5% of software engineering ads. Two percent of employers ever write it.
- It is worth nothing. Those ads advertise $205K against $190K, but that gap is which company, not which job. Compare ads inside the same company and it is minus 2.8%, statistically zero. "Ship fast" and "bias for action" land at plus 0.3%.
- What does pay is the thing being sneered at. Ads that name evaluation work pay 2.5% more inside the same employer, and beat the shipping language head to head.
- And the ads carrying the disclaimer are asking for research anyway. Among LLM postings, 53% want eval harnesses and offline evaluation, against 21% of everyone else. Inside AI Engineer, ads that disclaim research demand evaluation at the same rate as ads that don't: 41% versus 40%.

So, to whoever is writing this into job descriptions: you are not filtering for pragmatism. You are filtering out the people who would have told you the model regressed.

Shipping a probabilistic system without evaluation is shipping without tests. Evals, experimentation and prototyping are not a researcher's indulgence. They are the release process. The market already prices them above your swagger.
