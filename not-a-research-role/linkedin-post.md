If you've started hiding the research on your resume because AI job ads keep saying "this is not a research role," put it back.

We pulled 62,659 US tech job postings and looked for that line and its relatives: "not just papers," "beyond the notebook," "this is not a play-with-models-in-a-lab role."

It's rare. About 3% of AI Engineer ads use it, and only 2% of employers have ever written it.

It doesn't pay. Ads with the line advertise $205K against $190K, but that gap comes from which companies write it. Compare ads within the same company and the premium disappears. "Ship fast" and "bias for action" are worth nothing either.

The ads using it want research anyway. Among LLM job ads, 53% of the ones with the disclaimer ask for eval harnesses or offline evaluation. Only 21% of the rest do.

And the one phrase that does carry a premium inside the same company is evaluation work, at about 2.5%.

To the people writing these ads: the worry behind the line is fair. Nobody wants a hire whose work never leaves a notebook. But the line doesn't screen for that. It tells the person who would build your eval set that they aren't welcome, a few paragraphs before your posting asks for an eval set.

If you ship LLM features, evaluation is how you find out a prompt change broke something before a customer does. If you need someone who owns the system in production, say that. Research isn't the problem.
