# LinkedIn post 1 (Lenny's evals angle, post now)

Everyone says PMs need to learn evals. The job postings agree, just not the way you'd expect.

Mike Krieger calls evals "probably the most important thing" to teach product people, and Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar's new guide in Lenny's Newsletter makes the case in detail. So I checked what employers are actually writing, across 63,316 US job postings.

- 1 in 7 PM postings for GenAI products now ask for AI evals. That's the same rate as data scientists.
- The ask doubled in four months, from 2.7% to 5.8% of PM postings at the same employers. Data scientists stayed flat.
- It's hands-on. 79% of PM eval asks want you doing the work, and 46% ask the PM to write the evals.

The twist: Husain and Shankar say error analysis (reading traces, naming the failure modes) is the one step to prioritize if you do nothing else. Employers include it in 38% of data-science eval asks and only 20% of PM ones.

And almost no PM lists evals on their profile yet: 5 of 2,202 recent PM positions as of late 2025.

If you're a PM on an AI product, writing evals is now a job requirement. Error analysis is where you can get ahead of it.

# LinkedIn post 2 (data-science angle, recycle the same blog another day)

Data scientists think evaluation is their home turf on AI teams. Employers are quietly handing it to product managers.

Across 63,316 US job postings, here is who gets asked for AI evals:

- Employers posted 2.5x more eval-asking PM roles than data-science roles (442 vs 175).
- Among postings for GenAI work, PMs and data scientists are asked at the same rate: about 1 in 7.
- Over four months the PM ask doubled. The data-science ask didn't move.

So "I know evals" is no longer a data-science differentiator. What still is: diagnosis. When a posting asks for evals, it assigns error analysis (reading model outputs, finding and naming the failure modes) in 38% of data-science roles versus 20% of PM roles. Data scientists are also more often asked to build the golden datasets (32% vs 20%).

The PM version of the eval job is writing the evals and setting the quality bar. The data-science version is figuring out why the system fails.

If you're a data scientist on an AI team, lead with the diagnosis, not the metric.
